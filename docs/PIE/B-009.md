# PIE — B-009: defense timing on world time

**Branch:** `fix/B-009-defense-world-time` (in `dev`).
**Already proven:**
- The editor builds, and 35/35 tests pass.
- The new `WoR.Defense.Clock` tests show that the defense clock is world time, and that real time passing with no world tick (what a pause does) doesn't drain the press window.
- A player press is stamped on that clock.
- An AI press anchored to an early impact keeps its time.

**What changed for the player:** before, the press window was measured on the wall clock, but the timer that closes it ran on world time. Pausing could eat the window, and the two could disagree. Now both use world time. If the game runs slowed down (time dilation, not used today), the windows stretch with it.
**Setup:** open the editor on `dev` and start a fight where an enemy attacks you.

| # | Do this | You should see | Result |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Let an enemy attack and block or parry on the hit, as you normally would | Timing feels the same as before: a good press still gets Perfect or Good, a late one still misses | [ ] |
| 2 | Watch an AI party member (or enemy) defend | It still blocks, parries or dodges sometimes, and it isn't always perfect | [ ] |
| 3 | During an enemy attack, press PIE **Pause** (toolbar, or the Pause key) just before the hit lands. Wait a few seconds, then **Resume** and press defend on the hit | The window is still there after resuming: your press counts and isn't treated as late | [ ] |
| 4 | Do a multi-hit attack (a combo or a projectile spell) on yourself and defend each hit | Each hit is judged on its own, as before | [ ] |

**Note:** world time only changes once per frame, so a press and a hit in the same frame count as exactly on time. This was true in practice before too, because input is read once per frame. On **Impossible**-tier hits the late margin is about one frame, so a press one frame late misses. Say if Impossible now feels unfair.
