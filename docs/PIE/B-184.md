# PIE — B-184: enemy teams on the map

**Branch:** `feature/B-184-field-enemy-teams` (in `dev`).
**Already proven:** the editor builds and every test passes. `WoR.Trial.FieldEnemyTeam` (4 cases) checks four things: a caught team fights whole, several send their strongest each, at most three; a win beats every caught team; a beaten team is removed when the level loads again; a team walks its patrol.

**What changed:** you can place an enemy team on a trial map (`FieldEnemyTeam`, a grey cylinder for now). It fights the team in its encounter data. A team you beat stays gone for the rest of the run; going back to the hub resets it. This also applies to the enemy pawns already placed with an encounter: after you win, they no longer come back when the trial level reloads. Teams standing near the fight join in, each sending its strongest member (Q-184).

**Setup:** a trial level. Place two `FieldEnemyTeam` actors far apart. On each, select its **Encounter** component and set **Encounter Data** to a team; use three enemies for the first. Give the second two or three **Patrol Points** (drag the diamond widgets). Output Log open.

| # | Do this | You should see | Result |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Play; watch the second team | It walks its points and back; the first stands still | [ ] |
| 2 | Run `wor.Trial.PrintTeams` | One line per team: its team size, "idle" or "patrols N point(s)" | [ ] |
| 3 | Walk into the first team | Join window, then a 1 v 3 fight against its three members | [ ] |
| 4 | Win | Back in the trial, the first team is gone. Output Log: "[EncounterComponent] ... was beaten earlier this run - removed" | [ ] |
| 5 | Walk into the second team and flee (or lose) | Back in the trial it is still there | [ ] |
| 6 | Move the two teams next to each other (within about 8 m), play, touch one | The fight has one enemy from each team (the strongest of each); win, and both are gone | [ ] |
| 7 | Return to the hub, enter the trial again | Every team is back | [ ] |
