# Tier Power

**Status:** Live (pending final balance pass). Player-facing reference.

## Concept

A thing's **tier** (F up to S) sets how strong it is. **F is the baseline**; every tier above it
is stronger, all the way up to S, which is roughly **4.8× as powerful as an F** of the same thing.
This is separate from how well your gear *matches* what you're using (that's the channel-match
rule, below) — tier power is simply *how strong it was built*.

## Skills (spells & abilities)

A skill's tier scales its **damage, its status build-up, and its cost** by one factor — all
together, in the same proportion. A higher-tier skill hits harder and builds status faster, but
also **costs more** (stronger skills are more expensive — same direction). It does **not** change
the skill's special effects (burns, stuns, buffs, and the like stay as authored).

Because everything scales together, raising a skill's tier is a clean upgrade: the same skill,
turned up — same feel, bigger numbers, proportional cost.

| Tier  | F     | E     | D     | C     | B     | A     | S     |
| ----- | ----- | ----- | ----- | ----- | ----- | ----- | ----- |
| Power | ×1.00 | ×1.30 | ×1.70 | ×2.20 | ×2.85 | ×3.70 | ×4.80 |

## Stacks with the channel-match rule

Tier power and the **gap** (channel match) are two different questions and they multiply together:

- **Tier power** — how strong the thing is built (this page).
- **Gap** — whether the gear you're channelling it through can carry it. A weak catalyst forcing
  a strong action is penalized; a strong catalyst lifting a weak action is boosted.

So a high-tier spell pushed through under-matched gear is still strong from its tier, but loses
some of that to the gap — and vice versa.

## Gear

Tier changes **how much each stat point is worth**, not how many points you roll:

- **Every tier rolls the same number of stat points now.** An S item doesn't get more points than
  an F item — but each of its points is **worth more**. Higher tier = stronger per point.
- **Cursed (negative) rolls hit harder at higher tier too.** Because tier scales the *value* of
  each point in both directions, a high-tier item with a bad roll has a **bigger downside** as
  well as a bigger upside. High-tier gear is higher-stakes: more to gain, more to lose.
- **HP / Energy gear got a real boost.** Health and Energy bonuses from gear used to be tiny;
  they now add a meaningful amount per point (and stack on top of your normal pool cap).

## Quick gut-check

- An S skill ≈ 4.8× an F skill's damage/status — and costs ≈ 4.8× as much.
- Tiers in between climb steadily (~30% per step early, widening toward S).
- A great roll on an S item is excellent; a cursed roll on an S item is genuinely punishing.
- Tier is *power built in*; the gap is *whether your gear keeps up*. They both apply.
